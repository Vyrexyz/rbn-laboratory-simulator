Regulatory Network Notebook

A working specimen of a genetic regulatory network, modelled as a Random Boolean Network — each gene reads the expression of k regulators, combined through l-way hyperedges, and switches according to a randomly assigned rule. Observe how synchronous, asynchronous, and stochastic update regimes reshape the same wiring (Gershenson, 2002), probe order, chaos, and the critical edge (Gershenson, 2004), and see how higher-order interactions among regulators reshape attractor dynamics (Stoltz & Joslyn, 2024) — all built on Kauffman's (1969, 1993) model of cell differentiation.

Every gene is wired through k regulatory hyperedges, each combining l regulator genes via a random Boolean function before feeding the gene's own rule — the higher-order construction of Stoltz & Joslyn (2024), which reduces to the classical Kauffman N‑K‑p model at l=1. Preferential-attachment wiring produces a few heavily-regulating hub genes, echoing the scale-free topologies observed in real regulatory networks (Gershenson, 2004).

Derrida plot order · chaos, empirically
Samples many pairs of states at each starting Hamming distance H₀, applies one synchronous step to both, and plots the resulting distance H₁. Below the diagonal, perturbations shrink — the ordered regime; above it, they grow — chaotic. This is the Derrida & Pomeau (1986) diagnostic, measured directly on this specimen rather than assumed from k and p alone.
H₀ (initial distance) vs. mean H₁ (distance after one step). Dashed line is H₁=H₀.
Cell fate map multistability
The same genome — identical wiring and rules — started from four different random expression patterns and run to their attractors under CRBN. Kauffman's central claim: a genome's cell types are simply its distinct attractors, reached from different starting conditions, not different genomes.
